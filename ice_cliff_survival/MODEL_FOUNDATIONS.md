# MODEL_FOUNDATIONS — hydraulic control of ice-cliff survival and reburial (milestone 0)

Status: mathematical foundations and a reduced structural baseline. Every result in §6–§7 is a **consistency result of standard ODE/conservation mathematics** (no historical originality claimed); §2–§5 are balance laws; §8 lists what is assumed. Units SI; ice density ρ_i = 917, water ρ_w = 1000 kg m⁻³, L_f = 3.34×10⁵ J kg⁻¹, c_p = 4217 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹. Code references are to `ice_cliff_survival/`.

## 0. Frames, signs, notation
* Glacier-fixed frame: coordinates attached to the (slowly moving, here assumed stagnant) ice; the debris-covered surface lowers by sub-debris melt (Evatt et al. 2024 use this moving frame; here it only changes how upper-mantle thickness d_top and the surface elevation evolve).
* **Interface:** Γ(t), unit normal **n pointing from ice into water/debris/air**, interface velocity v_Γ, ice material velocity u_ice, water velocity u_w. Recession speed m_n := (u_ice − v_Γ)·n ≥ 0 (melting).
* **Toe control volume (CV)** Ω_toe(t): the region adjacent to the cliff face and toe containing the stream and the deposited clasts; its boundary moves with velocity w(x,t). Grain flux across a boundary element is c (u_s − w)·n_in dA with n_in the inward normal (`grains.relative_flux`); a frozen grain has u_s = u_ice.
* Grain concentration is always **solid volume per mixture volume** c_mix unless stated; pure-ice basis c_ice = c_mix/(1−c_mix) (`grains.c_mix_from_c_ice`). Deposited debris has porosity φ: V_bulk = V_s/(1−φ). **All stocks and fluxes below are solid volume** (pore space excluded).

## 1. Water/air flow and heat
Incompressible laminar/RANS or resolved flow in the stream; free surface by VOF; sensible heat Θ = T − T_m:
ρ_w c_p(∂_tΘ + u·∇Θ) = ∇·(k∇Θ) + Φ_visc, with Φ_visc the physical viscous dissipation, **counted once**: either resolved in the flow solver or supplied by a reach-scale friction law, never both, and numerical diffusion is not added as heat. Integral form on a CV moving with w: dE_w/dt = P_in − P_out + P_atm + Φ − P_w, E_w = ∫ρ_w c_pΘ dV, P_in/out = ∮ρ_w c_pΘ (u−w)·n dA over open boundaries, P_w = ∮_Γ q_w dA the power delivered to the ice **(the same number removed from the water and used for melting)**. Water created at Γ enters at Θ = 0, so it dilutes but adds no sensible heat. Reference implementations: `ice_network_v2/foam/interThermalFoam` (passive sensible heat, both phases, no buoyancy, no phase change) and `ice_network_v2/predict.py` (reach model).

## 2. Moving ice boundary
Mass jump (Rankine–Hugoniot on Γ): ρ_i(u_ice − v_Γ)·n = ρ_w(u_w − v_Γ)·n ⇒ **(u_w − v_Γ)·n = (ρ_i/ρ_w) m_n**. For stationary ice (u_ice = 0): v_Γ·n = −m_n and **u_w·n = (ρ_i/ρ_w − 1) m_n** (slightly negative: the denser meltwater occupies less volume, so liquid is drawn toward Γ); the equal-density limit gives u_w·n = 0 (`interface.water_normal_velocity`, tested). Energy jump: **ρ_i L_f m_n = k_w ∂_nT_w − k_i ∂_nT_i** (heat arriving from the water side minus heat conducted into the ice). *Temperate ice* (T_i ≡ T_m) has ∂_nT_i = 0, so ρ_i L_f m_n = k_w∂_nT_w and the sensible-plus-latent ledger of the ice side collapses to latent only; cold ice adds a conduction field and a heat-up energy. **Covered/dry interface:** where debris lies between the ice and the stream/air, the water term is replaced by the heat conducted through the debris (supplied as a flux q_deb; `interface.covered_interface_melt`); stream power does not reach the ice there, so a covered face receives atmospheric/debris-conducted melt only, which Petersen et al. and Evatt et al. show is much smaller than exposed-face melt for thick debris. Verified against the exact Neumann solution (`tests/test_geometry_interface.py`).

## 3. Grains: frozen, mobile, deposited
* **Frozen (englacial)** concentration c_mix in the ice; released when the face recedes: sweeping mixture volume A r_n (r_n the geometric normal recession speed of the mixture) releases **S_eng = c_mix A r_n** [m³ s⁻¹ solid]. Equivalent to the relative-flux integral with u_s = u_ice = 0 and w·n_in = −r_n (`grains.englacial_source`; tested against independent volume bookkeeping). Pure-ice melt rate ρ_i(1−c_mix)A r_n.
* **Upper mantle:** a debris layer of bulk thickness d_top and porosity φ sits above the crest, the crest retreats at speed v_top **(not equal to toe incision in general: d(x_crest − x_toe)/dt = d(H cot θ)/dt, which vanishes only for parallel retreat)**; **S_mantle = (1−φ) W d_top v_top f_deliv**, with f_deliv the fraction reaching the toe CV; (1−f_deliv) is a recorded sink, not an external source (`grains.mantle_split`). Material released from the mantle **must not also appear in S_ext**; `ToeStock.step(..., mantle_double_counted_in_ext=…)` removes and reports it.
* **Toe stock** (solid): **dV_s/dt = S_up + S_mantle + S_eng + S_ext − Q_out**; S_up delivered by the stream from upstream, S_ext other external inputs (e.g. collapse of the face not already counted), Q_out the actual export, which must satisfy Q_out ≤ capacity(Q, d, …) and V_s ≥ 0 with the ACTUAL clipped flux recorded.
* **Mobile vs deposited:** clast-scale mobilisation (§ transport) splits the stock into a part that the stream can move (capacity) and a part it cannot (armour). A stock threshold alone does not establish burial: coverage depends on the pile geometry (`geometry.py`: wedge height z_f = √(2A/(cot β − cot θ)), fully covered at z_f ≥ H), and survival on stream contact and export, not on V_s.

## 4. Exposure classes and melt partition
For the face of area A_face: **covered** (z ≤ z_f: no stream or atmospheric exposure; only debris-conducted melt), **exposed dry** (above the pile and above the water: atmospheric energy balance), **exposed wetted** (above the pile, below the stream/pond level: water-driven melt P_w plus atmospheric terms on the dry part), **submerged** (below water and not covered). Stream–ice contact loss = decrease of A_wetted,exposed = W·max(0, min(h_w,H) − z_f)/sinθ in the reduced geometry (structural assumption: water inside the pile pores does not count as ice contact). Melt energy: stream-driven M_str = P_w/(ρ_iL_f) vs atmospheric M_atm from the surface energy balance on the dry exposed area; they are tracked in separate ledgers and never summed with a shared coefficient.

## 5. Independent ledgers
1. **Grain (solid volume):** Σ recorded sources − Σ recorded export − ΔV_s = 0 (`ToeStock.budget_residual`, `budgets.grain_budget`: recomputed from the recorded series without calling the stepper); plus mantle and englacial inventories decreasing by exactly the amounts released.
2. **Water/ice mass:** ρ_i V_ice,melted = ρ_w V_meltwater,added (`cfd_contract.ExchangeRecord.validate`).
3. **Sensible + latent energy:** water side Σ(P_in + Φ − P_out − P_w)Δt − ΔE_w = 0; ice side ρ_iL_f V_ice,melted = ∫P_w dt (temperate), reported **separately** from the first so a discrete closure never hides a latent-heat inconsistency (the milestone-3 Stefan lesson: discrete ledger 1e-14…1e-12 vs physical sensible+latent residual 4.25e-4 at dt = 2.5 s, first order in dt).

## 6. Mathematical falsifiers (restricted scalar model; `scalar_model.py`)
Model: R = F(T,s), ds/dt = S₀ + D F(T,s) − C(s,Q), D>0 constant, Q fixed. Hypotheses (H): F_T>0, F_s<0, C_s≥0, C(0,Q)=0, C¹. Let g(s;T) = S₀ + D F − C.
**(a)** g_s = D F_s − C_s < 0, so g is strictly decreasing in s: on a connected admissible interval **at most one** root exists (existence needs g(s_lo) ≥ 0 ≥ g(s_hi), checked separately) and it is locally (exponentially) stable with rate |g_s|.
**(b)** Implicit differentiation g_T + g_s ds*/dT = 0 gives **ds*/dT = D F_T/(C_s − D F_s) > 0**, and R* = F(T,s*): **dR*/dT = F_T + F_s ds*/dT = F_T C_s /(C_s − D F_s) ≥ 0** (equality iff C_s = 0). Exposed-melt response saturates (→ 0 as C_s → 0 is the *transport-unlimited* limit where all extra melt becomes stock) but **cannot become negative** under (H). If C_s < 0 with C_s − D F_s > 0 the sign reverses (necessity of C_s ≥ 0; tested).
**(c) Cumulative restriction.** Same s(0), Q(t), S₀(t), constant D, T_H(t) ≥ T_L(t). Since F_T>0, F(T_H,s) ≥ F(T_L,s) for each s, so the right-hand side for H dominates that for L pointwise; for a C¹ scalar ODE the comparison theorem gives s_H(t) ≥ s_L(t). Subtracting the two integrated equations: s_H − s_L = D∫(R_H − R_L)dt − ∫[C(s_H,Q) − C(s_L,Q)]dt, hence **D∫(R_H − R_L)dt = (s_H − s_L) + ∫[C(s_H) − C(s_L)]dt ≥ 0** because C_s ≥ 0 and s_H ≥ s_L. Assumptions: trajectories remain in the admissible interval (guaranteed if C(0)=0, S₀ ≥ 0, no upper wall), no clipping — clipping a negative stock without recording the changed flux changes the equations and voids the argument (`integrate` raises instead of clipping).
**Consequences.** Simple transport limitation can produce saturation but **not a negative cumulative warming response**; a reversal requires an independently justified additional state or dependence (changing exposure/contact area, export capacity depending on temperature, geometry feedback, D depending on s or T). The model therefore falsifies a "reversal from transport limitation alone" claim, and says nothing about whether the extra states exist.

## 7. Accumulation bound
If supply ≥ S_min and the admissible (hence the actual) export ≤ C_max < S_min for all t, then V_s(t) ≥ V_s(0) + (S_min − C_max)t. A fixed stock V_crit is therefore reached no later than **t_hit ≤ (V_crit − V_s0)/(S_min − C_max)** provided the bounds persist (`scalar_model.accumulation_hitting_bound`; tested with random bounded forcing and equality for constant forcing). It is an **upper** hitting-time bound — not a minimum time, and not proof of burial (V_crit must be a geometric covering volume (`geometry.critical_solid_volume`) *and* the stream must fail to re-expose the face).

## 8. What is assumed (not derived)
Planar face, quasi-2-D cross-section; one stream-contact length; the debris-wedge geometry with a fixed repose angle; pore water not an ice contact; closure forms for export (force balance + an assumed clearing model, `transport.py`) have **no calibration**; no ponds; no mechanical collapse; no thermal buoyancy; ice at the melting point for stream-driven melt.
