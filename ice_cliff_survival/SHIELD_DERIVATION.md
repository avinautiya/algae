# Static debris shield: what the Robin condition assumes (M2)

**Boundaries.** (i) Physical ice boundary: x=0, temperate ice, T=T_m (=0 in the solver's temperature reference); melts when heat arrives. (ii) Liquid/debris boundary: x=−d(z), the top of the pile; liquid temperature there T_ℓ. (iii) Between them a debris layer of normal thickness d (corrected formula in `geometry.debris_normal_thickness`), conductivity k_d, treated as **eliminated**: massless, so steady conduction gives the flux into the ice q = k_d (T_ℓ − T_m)/d = h T_ℓ, h = k_d/d. In the solver (mixed BC, valueFraction f = h/(h+kδ)) the liquid sees a conductance h to an isothermal ice face; the interface then moves with ρ_i L ṡ = h T_ℓ.

**Enthalpy reference and where meltwater enters.** Enthalpy is measured as ρ_w c_w (T − T_m) for liquid, with the latent term carried by the interface condition. Melt is produced *at the ice boundary at T_m* (phase equilibrium), so its sensible enthalpy relative to the reference is c_w(T_m−T_m)=0: the advected enthalpy flux through the ice patch is zero by construction of the reference, independent of any ledger. With a debris layer the water would have to pass through the pores to reach the liquid; that transport (volume flux (ρ_i/ρ_w)·melt rate, ≈ 1e-8–1e-7 m s⁻¹ ) is neglected: the Péclet number ρ_w c_w v d / k_d ≈ 4e6·3e-8·7e-3/0.5 ≈ 2e-3 ≪ 1, so heat carried by it changes the conductance by ≲0.2 %. (Not a ledger-closure device; the M1 ledger found the zero-flux condition necessary, and this is why it is the correct one.)

**Neglected.** Debris heat capacity (and pore water phase change/freezing), lateral conduction in the wedge (k/d is the 1-D normal-resistance limit; wedge angle between face and pile surface of order θ−β is not resolved), pore convection, change of d as the interface recedes or the pile settles, contact resistance, ice-side conduction (temperate ice).

**When the massless resistance is adequate.** Resolved planar two-material problem (`shield_planar.py`, step T_w at the pile top, ice face at T_m, initially isothermal): the heat delivered to the ice lags the massless value by the storage deficit ρc_d d T_w/6 (J m⁻²) at late time, i.e. a relative cumulative deficit → t_lag/t with t_lag = ρc_d d²/(6 k_d). Verified: analytic series vs independent Crank–Nicolson FV (≤1e-5). Examples (k_d=0.5, ρc_d=2.5e6; `cfd/results/shield_planar.json`): d=2 mm t_lag 3.3 s; d=7 mm 41 s (1.1 % deficit after 1 h, 6.8 % after 10 min); d=2 cm 333 s (9 % after 1 h); d=10 cm 8300 s (deficit 97 % after 1 h: **the massless approximation fails**). Rule of thumb: adequate (<5 % cumulative) when t > 20 t_lag; debris storage matters for thick piles, diurnal/short forcing, or high-ρc saturated debris. These inputs are assumed, not measured.
