# Coupling audit

Read-only review of `environment_extension_2026_11_01`; no existing source was changed. The reviewed components can support a restricted, executable environmental sensitivity model. They do not establish novelty, field accuracy, cliff stability, or collapse prediction.

## Minimum executable interface

Add an independent `coupled.py` driver with `step(state, forcing, parameters, dt_s) -> (state, ledger)` and a JSON CLI. State must contain the `SteppedCliff`, named surface patches, extensive cold reserves in J, extensive debris sensible enthalpy in J relative to 0 C, debris heat capacities in J/K, and cumulative melt in kg. Forcing must name route inflows, inlet water superheat, downstream stage, signed atmospheric patch fluxes, debris elevations, and any explicit exposure/remapping policy. Require all physical parameters and provenance; synthetic examples must be labeled synthetic.

1. Freeze geometry and patch areas at the beginning of the thermal step. Rebuild route floors from the actual current ice floor and solve `solve_common_stage` on disjoint routes. Its solution is quasi-steady; it has no pond-volume storage. Do not treat its hydraulic residual as a transient water balance.
2. Map each route discharge to explicitly identified contact patches. Bypass water must bypass ice heat transfer unless a contact geometry is supplied. Derive stage/debris face fractions with `face_partition`; assign floor and face actual extruded areas separately.
3. Call `water_heat_rates` once for each independent water parcel/route, using its inlet heat and serial floor-to-face conductances. Partition the returned floor/face W among exclusive contact patches. Water delivered to ice must sum to those returned rates, never exceed incoming sensible heat, and never be reintroduced as a separate `rho*cp*Q*superheat` source. Do not give every parallel route the whole inflow. Head-loss dissipation from `filled_slot_discharge` is not automatically ice heat.
4. Convert extensive thermal states to the fixed-area API immediately before each call: `cold_j_m2=C_J/A`, `debris_temperature_c=H_J/K_J`, `debris_capacity_j_m2_k=K_J/A`; use the appropriate areal conductance. Divide allocated water W by the same A. Skip zero-area patches without discarding their stored energy silently. Call `step_bare_ice` or `step_column`, then multiply every returned per-area energy by that same beginning-step A exactly once. Reset or disregard the API's cumulative `melted_kg_m2` and accumulate extensive melt separately.
5. Sum only returned latent J by face and floor; pass these to `advance_latent` with the same density and latent heat. Face-first then floor removal is already volume-consistent. Do not multiply floor latent energy by the post-retreat floor area: that area is used only by the geometry routine to turn an already extensive energy into lowering.
6. Explicitly remap thermal states and debris capacity onto updated patches, then report forcing, water, thermal, remapping, phase and volume ledgers. Return the new state; do not mutate the reviewed modules.

## Changing-area storage is the main coupling hazard

Keeping `cold_reserve_j_m2`, debris temperature, and areal capacity unchanged while area grows creates cold reserve and debris energy/capacity. Conversely, shrinking a patch silently destroys them. The thermal substep is conservative only on its frozen area; it does not settle the remapping physics.

For a minimal declared surface-following reservoir model, conserve extensive cold reserve C, debris enthalpy H, and capacity K during a purely numerical area redistribution. After redistribution, set areal quantities from the new area. A changed debris temperature is warranted only by changed H/K. Split or merge patches by explicit overlap weights and transfer both H and K. New physical debris requires an explicit mass/capacity and temperature source. Removing debris exports its signed sensible enthalpy and capacity. Wet/dry or debris/bare reclassification must transfer or export states explicitly; it cannot reset cold reserve without a ledger term.

Conserving a surface reservoir alone does **not** model fresh cold ice exposed by retreat. If newly exposed material is intended to carry cold content, prescribe a volumetric cold enthalpy and a represented active depth/material rule, account for its enthalpy import and any retired material, and document the additional closure. A lumped reservoir transported with the moving surface is an alternative reduced assumption, but cannot be described as a resolved cold-ice column. Require an exposure policy rather than inferring it from area differences. Geometric area growth is not itself the full newly exposed material area.

Signed storage convention: define system thermal enthalpy `U = H - C`. Each frozen-area thermal step must satisfy `atmospheric_J + water_J = delta(U) + latent_J`. Across a geometry/remapping step include explicit signed enthalpy imports/exports. Track remap residuals separately; an internally closed ledger does not validate the chosen exposure model.

## Scope and rejection rules

- `face_partition` assigns covered face to debris even under water. `step_column` puts `water_w_m2` directly into ice, bypassing debris. Reject submerged-debris contact in the minimal driver unless a separately specified water-to-debris boundary is implemented and its energy is allocated once. Atmospheric heating is not an appropriate default for submerged debris. Bare-water patches should normally receive no atmospheric surface flux.
- `water_heat_rates` assumes exchange with a 0 C boundary. It can feed the lumped cold-reserve bookkeeping but does not resolve heat exchange against a subfreezing surface. Likewise, `step_column` conducts to a 0 C reference despite outstanding cold content. State this limitation, and do not claim a cold-ice temperature solution. `atmospheric_flux` accepts surface temperatures only from 0 to 50 C; a cold bare-ice temperature cannot be supplied through that helper.
- `advance_latent` spreads each energy total uniformly over its planar face/floor. Patch-resolved heating therefore supports only an area-averaged geometry response, not an undercut. Reject finite-domain exhaustion or adapt/subdivide to an explicit terminal event; do not silently clip energy or create negative geometry.
- Keep the actual route floor; a low leak coefficient does not authorize passage through intact ice. Use the aperture, Darcy, and filled-slot closures only within their stated geometry/regimes. The slot solver is separate from common-stage route ratings; using it in that solver requires an explicit monotone rating adapter, not a relabeled orifice coefficient.
- Specify which geometry quantities remain fixed as the floor lowers (debris top elevation, debris thickness, aperture roof, downstream stage). Those choices change contact and discharge and cannot be selected implicitly.

## Required verification before coupling claims

Check zero forcing and zero flow; positive/negative atmospheric heat; initially cold bare ice; warm/cold debris release/storage; exclusive patch-area sums; water heat allocation and outlet balance; no-contact bypass; cold repayment before melt; area growth with nonzero states; patch disappearance/reclassification; explicit fresh-ice/debris imports; finite-domain failure; and `rho_i * L * polygon_volume_loss == face_latent_J + floor_latent_J`. Include a deliberate double-water-heat regression and a test that remapping cannot manufacture storage without a recorded source. Test refinement of dt with moving areas and changing contact, including face/floor splitting sensitivity. Report conservation tolerances and prediction convergence separately. Field applicability still requires site-specific geometry, coincident hydraulic/thermal forcing, and an independent comparison target.
