# Numerical blocker: stalls near first overtopping (red_C009_r9 and relatives)

Reproduction (`repro_stall.py red_C009_r9`, bounded by an evaluation cap): the frozen challenge run stalled at simulated t about 1450 s (first overtopping of sill 1; head over the sill 6.7e-5 m) with LSODA steps of order 1e-5 s.
Evidence at the stalled state (all in the script output): (i) time does advance, in steps of 1e-5 s, so this is step-size collapse, not an infinite loop; (ii) at heads 6.70-6.80e-5 m the *raw* root Q(H) of the entrance equation is not monotone (2.44e-7, 2.44e-7, 1.70e-7, 2.23e-7, 2.23e-7, 1.86e-7, 2.44e-7, 2.03e-7, ... m3/s for heads 1e-7 m apart): the multiple-root structure of the micro-depth regime documented in `ice_network/docs/root_multiplicity.md` (depth about 7e-5 m, below the 1 mm floor); (iii) the 1 mm activation floor scales Q by x^2(3-2x) with x = h_c/1 mm about 0.02, so the floored discharge is only about 1e-10 m3/s and its fluctuation is invisible to the volume states; (iv) but the **cumulative heat and dissipation states** (J) were integrated with the same absolute tolerance as the volumes, 1e-9, i.e. one nanojoule: the heat-to-ice rate in segment 0 fluctuates by 3e-4 to 1e-3 W between neighbouring heads (e.g. 5.5e-4 W against 1.3e-3 W), so keeping |error| < 1 nJ forces steps below 1e-5 s; the finite-difference sensitivity of that component to the lake volume changes sign with the perturbation size (3.7e-3, -1.8e3, -34, 0.28 W/m3 for dV = 1e-9 ... 1e-3 m3).
Root selection and event handling: the largest-root rule is unchanged (a coherent branch cannot be defined where the raw law has several roots separated by less than the dyadic bracketing factor of 2; changing it would be a different regularisation of an unphysical regime), and no event location is needed because the stall is not a missed event.
**Correction implemented (opt-in, version-flagged): `Network.run(..., atol_energy=1e-3)`**: per-component absolute tolerance, 1e-3 J on the cumulative energy-type states (sensible heat out, external heat in, dissipation, segment heat, wall heat), volumes keep 1e-9 m3. 1 mJ melts 3e-9 m3 of ice, i.e. nanometres of incision per segment; it is a change of error *weighting*, not a clipping or a floor on any physical quantity. Default `None` keeps the legacy behaviour, so every frozen result remains reproducible.
Verification (`blocker_test.py`, `records_blocker.json`): red_C009_r9 completes in 4.2 s (4 730 law evaluations; stalled before), red_C019_r9 completes in 6.6 s (stalled at t = 6300 s before), red_C031_r17 completes in 16.2 s (previously "Unexpected istate in LSODA" at t = 14 850 s); water and energy residuals 1e-15 and 0. The scope assertion of red_C025 (sill incised below the lake bottom) is unaffected and remains a declared scope exclusion. Equivalence on the frozen scenarios (r17): S1 and S2 event times change by < 6e-6 s, sill lowering by < 1.3e-10 m, delivered volume by < 1.3e-7 m3, discharge by < 4e-9 of the inflow, while the number of law evaluations falls from 5852/5616 to 3388/3200 and the wall time from 9.7/9.9 s to 4.8/4.7 s.
**Consequence to be handled in a new assessment, not done here:** the frozen accuracy-cost results used the legacy tolerance; the corrected version halves the cost of the reduced model at identical solution, so the "no matched-accuracy advantage" verdict of the previous evaluation was obtained with a needlessly expensive integrator tolerance and must be re-assessed (versioned rerun of every affected scenario; the original results stay as published). The capture study below used the legacy tolerance for all of its network runs so that its numbers are comparable with the frozen evaluation.
