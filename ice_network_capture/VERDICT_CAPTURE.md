# Melt-driven competition between two outlets: verdict

Idealised two-outlet problems only (a lake with two channels on temperate ice; 12 h; the shared Manning, Dittus-Boelter and critical-brink closures; no field data; no real-glacier forecast). Branch `claude/ice-network-m1`; frozen evaluation left untouched (tag `ice-eval-v1-frozen`). Theory: `docs/capture_theory.md`; prior art: `docs/capture_prior_art.md`; numerical blocker: `docs/numerical_blocker.md`; code: `rtm.py`, `design.py`, `predict.py`, `freeze_predictions.py`, `score_test.py`, `figures_capture.py`; runs: `ice_network_eval/records/runs/cap_*` (inventory below). Figures: `figures/fig1`-`fig4`.

## What was done
1. Prior-art check (sections in `docs/capture_prior_art.md`). The single most relevant statement is in Schoof et al. (J. Fluid Mech. 2023, section 2 limitations): "two competing outflow channels are presumably prone to instability, with the larger channel persisting while the smaller is abandoned" - a stated presumption without derivation, for a dissipation-only incision law. SaDS (Hill & Dow; thesis chapter 4) and SHED contain no analysis of capture or stability (keyword search; not a proof). Raymond & Nolan (2000)/Kingslake et al. (2017) give a one-channel runaway criterion (seen only through a summary). Jarosch & Gudmundsson (2012) not read in full.
2. Theory (`docs/capture_theory.md`): exact kinematics for two outlets (K1-K3), the quasi-steady-lake phase line of the sill gap with an unstable capture threshold, the symmetric-pair growth rate, a replicator-type stability index, definitions separating ordinary memory, irreversible memory and bistability (no bifurcation or hysteresis claimed), and the reduced translation-invariant model RTM (three state variables, rates tabulated once from the production kernels).
3. Development: 16 configurations (symmetric/asymmetric, steady/pulsed, 0.5-2 C), 48 network runs. 4. Frozen test: 16 new configurations on both sides of the predicted boundaries, predictions and rules committed before any test run (`TEST_FREEZE.md`, commit 9e65de1), 78 runs: 48 network r17 (thermal, dissipation-only surrogate, fixed geometry), 6 network r33, 24 resolved-reference runs (6 informative cases, 200 and 400 cells, thermal and fixed). Runtime was estimated first (about 50 min on two workers) and no resolution was reduced. 5. One numerical blocker reproduced and corrected (`docs/numerical_blocker.md`).

## Inventory
Development 48/48 completed; test 78/78 completed (0 failed, 0 stalled, 0 timed out); blocker verification runs separate (3 previously failing frozen cases now complete under the corrected tolerance; the frozen challenge records are unchanged). Total network + reference wall time of the test set about 2 h of worker time. Records: `ice_network_eval/records/runs/cap_*`, `eval_dev.json`, `scores_test.json`, `scores_test_summary.json`.

Failures and caveats of the prediction (kept):
* **The quasi-steady-lake capture threshold failed as a 12 h predictor.** It places the sign change of the gap drift at 166 mm (geometry A) and 96.5 mm (geometry B); gaps just above (T02, 180 mm; T05, 115 mm) were predicted to open but the network and the reference close (T02: -31 mm network, -22 mm reference 400, -15 mm reference 200; T05: -13 mm both). RTM17 predicted the closing correctly at both (-13.3, -6.1 mm), but **RTM33 predicted +13.2 mm at T02 against -22.2 mm in the reference**: the boundary location moves with station count and the 12 h horizon, and cases near it are not reliably classified. On the far side (T03, T06) the simulated drift is near zero (+0.5, +2.4 mm) against predicted +59 and +14 mm, because outlet 2 is nearly inactive (end share 0.10 and 0.21 against predicted 0.03/0.10). The threshold remains an asymptotic statement about the quasi-steady phase line, not tested to its asymptote.
* Share at end of run: errors of -0.04 to -0.11 in the six small-lake cases T01-T06 (RTM17) and for T01, T02, T05 against the reference. The accumulated incision there is 1.3-1.5 times the head over the sill (translation-invariance is stretched); no further diagnosis was attempted.
* RTM is not independent physics: its agreement with network r17 (switching times 0-3 %, sills 1-4 %) tests the approximations only; RTM17 inherits the network's station error (R2 incision over-predicted against the reference, closing rate too fast in `fig4_trajectories.png`).
Comparators (against the thermal network r17, 16 configurations): fixed geometry and the dissipation-only surrogate both predict **no sill-gap drift** (|drift| < 2 mm in 16/16 cases) whereas the thermal network drifts by more than 2 mm in 15/16; they miss the switch in 5 of the 6 configurations where the thermal network switches (T07, T08, T09, T12, T16; T11 switches hydraulically in all three). Fixed geometry and dissipation-only incision are indistinguishable at 1-3 C (gap drift 0.0 / -0.3 mm against -50.8 mm thermal in T07). The thermal network (r17/r33) is as accurate as RTM against the reference; RTM costs about 2-3 s (two tabulations) + milliseconds against 10-30 s.
Linear-in-temperature scaling (network r17, gap drift): D08/D04/D07 at 0.5/1/2 C: -10.2 / -22.1 / -50.8 mm (development, used in building the theory); test T16 at 0.3 C: -5.9 mm (predicted -5.8 mm, switching predicted at 29.9 ks, simulated 29.2 ks; near-tie). Symmetric pair (T13): gap drift +4.2 mm (predicted +4.3), reference +4.1 mm; the symmetric gap widens as predicted (instability of the symmetric state under advected-heat incision), T14 (steep-short) +22.7 against predicted +26.3 mm.

## Answers
**A. What is mathematically new, if anything?** Very little that is certainly new. The kinematic identities (K1-K3) and the replicator form are elementary re-expressions; the single-channel runaway criterion and the presumption that of two outlets the larger persists are in the literature (Raymond & Nolan 2000, Kingslake et al. 2017, Schoof et al. 2023 section 2). Candidate contributions, none established as original: (i) the capture threshold gap at which two outlets incise equally while carrying unequal flows, with its independence from inflow temperature and lake size under advected-heat incision, (ii) the contrast between advected-heat incision (stabilising at fixed lake level, index -0.3...-0.5; destabilising once the lake follows the sills) and dissipation-driven incision (index +0.1...+0.4), (iii) the decomposition of an observed switch into a hydraulic (fixed-geometry) and an incision-driven part (5 of 6 switching configurations were incision-driven). The prior-art table lists sources not read; no priority claim is made.
**B. What does the theory predict beyond the existing simulator?** No new physics: it is built from the simulator's kernels. Beyond it, it gives (a) a closed-form partition of a switch into hydraulic and incision-driven parts, (b) an algebraic quantity (E1 - E2 at a given partition, the equal-incision partition) that says in which direction capture runs without integrating in time, (c) the scaling of the drift and incision with inflow temperature, and (d) a three-variable model that reproduces the network's switching time and incision to 1-3 % at about 3-10 times lower cost (it is not more accurate than the network).
**C. Does the prediction survive independent numerical comparison?** Partly. Switching occurrence (6/6), switching time (one case, -0.2 %), incision (6/6 within 9 %) and the sign of the gap drift (5/6) survive the comparison with the independent 1-D resolved reference; the end-of-run partition does not (2/6), the quasi-steady capture threshold does not survive as a 12 h predictor, and RTM33 misclassifies the one reference case near the predicted boundary (T02). Only six reference cases were run; the reference's own error (200 -> 400) is comparable to some of the differences tested.
**D. Next single justified milestone.** Resolve the boundary-case failure before anything environmental: a new, independent test of the capture boundary with the corrected integrator tolerance and a station-converged network (33-65 stations, where the R2 incision error vanishes), asking whether the sign change of the gap drift lies at the quasi-steady threshold in the limit of long horizon and small lake, using the resolved reference on 4-6 gaps bracketing the predicted threshold. Realistic environmental work (valid heat-transfer closure, 2-D routing, multiple outlets) depends on that boundary being predictable and on a validated closure.

## Strongest supported contribution
A verified reduction of two-outlet competition to the pair of effective incision rates (E1, E2) of the production kernels, with a demonstrated ability to reproduce the network's switching times (0-3 %) and incision (1-4 %) and the resolved reference's switching occurrence, incision (within 9 %) and gap-drift sign (5/6) at roughly 3-10 times lower cost than the network (about 3 s against 10-30 s), plus a documented numerical correction that removes three of the five previously failed or stalled challenge runs (the other two are the declared-scope assertion of C025) and halves the reduced model's cost at identical solution. It is not a demonstrated new capture theory.
