## What the proof packet contributes

The candidate mathematical contribution is the specific joint result: a meltwater-corrected cumulative thermal map; constructive sharp bounds on regional incision under local/total/prefix information; forcing-uniform spatial extremizers; and the resulting optimal worst-case forecast and threshold timing. Basic conservation, convexity arguments, interval minimax reasoning and inverse-problem ambiguity are established methods.

The proof and numerical validation are complete for the stated finite-dimensional conductance class. A statement that these are the first such results in mathematics or glaciology still requires a broader full-text review and expert scrutiny. It is legitimate to present the proved statements and explain their scope without claiming settled historical priority.

## Why this is not a substitute for the main physical study

The active CFD work is investigating spatially resolved heat flow and evolving ice. It can later justify/tighten the conductance class or show that the reduced assumptions fail. The sharp bounds are exact for their class, but that class may include profiles not realizable by one fixed Navier–Stokes geometry. Conservative envelopes do not imply that every extremizer is an environmental state.

No observed improvement over existing glacier predictions is reported here. Current results show a conditional information limit and an executable way to forecast within it. The stronger physical mechanism and transfer improvement remain questions for the verified model and independent data.
