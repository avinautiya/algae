# Independent novelty gate — debris arrangement and persistent water–ice contact loss

Repository snapshot: `avinautiya/algae`, branch `claude/integration-m4`, commit `05e7312e3bbf248acf9f646267a5b8ea06a8b32e`. Read-only audit; no worktree, solver, result or Claude process changed. The release decision memo and claim–evidence table were downloaded at that SHA. This review is a targeted novelty search, not a certificate that no relevant publication exists.

## Decision

**No-go for the current broad headline of a newly discovered environmental mechanism or a field-superior glacier model. Conditional go for a specific quantitative model contribution, not yet demonstrated to be historically original.** The most credible candidate is a finite-head, evolving-geometry criterion predicting the last time at which removal of an insulating obstruction can restore a prescribed amount of water–ice access. It must exceed a stronger topography-aware comparator, survive plausible transmission/thermal closures, and have its numerical uncertainty separated from closure uncertainty. Equal debris volume with different arrangements is insufficient by itself: geometry-sensitive routing makes such a distinction expected.

Neither more complexity nor agreement between a reduced model and its parent simulator establishes Nature-level significance. The public evidence reviewed here does not establish a new theorem, a new natural phenomenon, or environmental predictive superiority.

## Important missing comparator found in this audit

[Strickland, Covington & Gulley, *Controls on the growth of topographic depressions on debris-covered glaciers*](https://doi.org/10.22541/essoar.175647655.50202412/v1), posted29Aug2025. The inspected primary manuscript is labeled a preprint; a peer-reviewed successor was not located in this targeted pass. Its77-page PDF contains duplicated manuscript material; equations below refer to the first copy.

Its model evolves ice elevation and debris thickness using sub-debris melt, hillslope transport, stream incision and internal sink removal. Equation7 includes incision proportional to `−κ b0 A^0.5 ∇s`; equation8 evolves debris. Discussion explicitly identifies incision independent of debris thickness as a simplification. It lacks a separate water-depth/head state and finite-water sensible-heat transport in the inspected implementation. This provides a **specific model limitation to compare against**, not a new discovery by us.

The [archived authors' code](https://github.com/ryans1286/controls-on-depression-growth/tree/v1.0) is available through [Zenodo16881412](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16881412). `model_1a.py`, Git blob `4dc1ad762a7d981c6bdec0a3aad4696f9c4f63e3`, calls `FastscapeEroder(K_sp=K_sp*b_0, ...)` after routing on the ice surface. No water-temperature state or finite-head contact law appears in the inspected script. Archive12.64MB, MD5`e6271307dc4b77f679ceffa5bc9dd83d`. This code was read, not executed.

A fair comparison must distinguish a different scientific target from a failure: this landscape-evolution model was not designed to resolve short-time shallow-water obstruction removal. Reimplementing a deliberately mismatched model and declaring victory would not establish general superiority.

## Other literature risks

[Mejia et al.2025, TC19,6445](https://tc.copernicus.org/articles/19/6445/2025/) reports field-observed catchment merger through thermally incised divides. Thus thermal incision changing network connectivity is already an observed environmental process. Our potential contribution is a quantitative, qualified access/recovery criterion, not discovery of network reorganization.

[Verhaegen & Huybrechts2026](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025JF008748) was verified through the authors' institutional publication record, but not read fully in this pass. It couples multidimensional debris transport to higher-order3-D glacier flow. Do not claim first coupled debris/glacier dynamics. [DCG-MIP2026](https://tc.copernicus.org/articles/20/1895/2026/) further shows that multilayer conductive debris models are established; the new two-layer component test is an application/validation result, not a new conduction theory.

## Required go/no-go discriminator before another campaign

1. State the observable precisely: e.g. post-removal delivered-water fraction during a specified window, not cliff survival or regional melt.
2. Derive the criterion before simulation from finite hydraulic head, evolving ice elevation and heat-limited incision. Separate new reduction from the familiar fact that an incised bypass retains a topographic advantage.
3. Compare at least against an evolving-topography routing model plus thermal resistance, not solely fractional cover and fixed geometry. If a straightforward enhanced stream-power/topographic baseline predicts the same boundary equally well, the new contribution is limited.
4. Demonstrate the same criterion across different initial layouts, variable discharge/temperature and plausible transmission, not merely repeated removal times along one shared pre-removal trajectory.
5. Distinguish irreversible geometric memory from genuine hysteretic equilibria/bifurcations. Lack of recovery over21days does not prove permanent irreversibility.
6. Show a meaningful consequence, such as bounded differences in matched-support heat delivery/melt, without turning plan-view floor melt into vertical-cliff lifetime.
7. Keep the failed field-accuracy verdict and unverified porous closure visible. These do not disappear under a mechanism framing.

**Bottom line:** a narrow, falsifiable quantitative contribution remains possible. Neither complete originality nor Nature-level importance is established. The report's current recommendation to draft a mechanism paper before resolving this comparison is premature. Finish the equation-level novelty discriminator first; do not launch a broad production campaign to hunt favorable outcomes.
