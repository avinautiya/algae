# Does the order of forcing change drainage selection when the total water and sensible heat are identical?

Idealised two-outlet problems only (lake, two channels on temperate ice, shared Manning / Dittus-Boelter / critical-brink closures; no surface heat exchange; no field data). **Candidate result, not an established discovery; no real-glacier forecast claim.** Branch `claude/ice-network-m1`; previous frozen results untouched (tags `ice-eval-v1-frozen`, `ice-capture-v1`). Derivation `docs/sequence_theory.md`; overlap assessment `docs/sequence_prior_art.md`; frozen predictions and rules `FREEZE.md`, `TEST_PREDICTIONS.json/.md`; results `SCORES_TEST.md`, `scores_test.json`, `eval_dev.json`, `eval_test.json`; figures `figures/fig1`-`fig5`; code `forcing.py`, `rtm_seq.py`, `bracket.py`, `theory_eta.py`, `predict_seq.py`, `score_seq.py`, `report_seq.py`, `figures_seq.py`; runs `ice_network_eval/records/runs/seq_*`.

## Design (as run)
Episodes A (0.03 m3/s, warm) and B (0.09 m3/s, cold), or the other contrasts listed in `sdesign.py`; AB, BA and an averaged control with Q_bar and the **discharge-weighted** temperature T_bar; same initial state (lake 0.10 m above sill 1, lake temperature = T_bar, undisturbed channels); geometry never reset; 6 h common continuation at the mean forcing. Water and sensible-heat integrals agree between AB, BA and the control to 1e-13. Development: 10 configurations (90 network runs). Frozen test: 10 further configurations chosen from an RTM-only scan of 120 plausible configurations by predicted-effect stratum (low, medium, near-tie, high), network r17 (thermal and fixed geometry), r33 on three, resolved 1-D reference at 200 cells on six (400 cells on X04 and X07), closure multipliers on three, tolerance check on two: 112 runs, 109 completed, 2 reference timeouts, 1 network stall (F9). Runtime was estimated before launch (about 100 min on two workers) and was about 2 h. Cases away from the capture boundary (X01-X03, X06, X09, X10) and near it (X04, X05, X07, X08) are both included.

## Results (frozen thresholds: routing effect meaningful if destination flips, or |delta end share| >= 0.05, or |delta delivered share| >= 0.03, or switching occurrence differs; incision effect meaningful if sill lowering differs >= 15 % or heat to the ice >= 10 %; an effect is resolved if it exceeds 3x the numerical uncertainty)
* **Destination:** 0 of 10 test configurations (and 0 of 10 development, 0 of the 6 resolved-reference cases) change the dominant outlet between AB, BA and the averaged control; ties occur only in BA of X08 (share 0.525).
* **Routing effect size (AB - BA, end-of-run outlet-2 share):** 0.000-0.040 in the network (maximum X10), meaningful by the frozen rule only for X09 (delivered-share effect -0.071 in the network, -0.072 in the 200-cell reference, with the end share -0.035/-0.028); numerical uncertainty of the effect 0.0002-0.0008 (r17 vs r33, ref 200 vs 400), so every effect above 0.003 is resolved.
* **Switching time:** the first-crossing time of Q2 > Q1 differs between orders in the near-tie cases by up to 9.6 h (X08: 16.5 h in AB, 26.1 h in BA) and by 1.4-2.7 h in X04, X07; these are mostly forced responses to different early forcing, not persistent memory.
* **Persistence through the common continuation** (ratio of the share effect at the end to that at the start of the continuation): 0.63 (X04), 0.85 (X05), 0.87 (X10); 0.15 (X08); near zero or sign-reversed in the others.
* **Energy delivered to the channels:** differs by up to 18 % (X08 +17.9 %; X04 +12.6 %, X09 +12.0 %, X05 +11.1 %), although incoming water and heat are identical: the final lake temperature differs. Meaningful incision effect: predicted for X04, X05, X08; observed for X04, X05, X08, X09 (one miss: X09, predicted from the closed-form eta = 0.089 against 0.12-0.13 observed; the RTM_seq heat prediction itself was within 0.009 for all ten).
* **Mechanism ablations (network):** with fixed geometry the share effect is at most 0.001 in 8 of 10 cases (X07 +0.003; X08 +0.015, a pure reservoir/lake-level timing effect larger than the thermal one); with dissipation-only incision (development) it is <= 0.001 except the large-lake case S03. The partition effect therefore needs thermal incision: lake thermal storage -> different heat delivered (closed form eta) -> different incision -> different partition.
* **Prediction outside development:** sign of the share effect correct in 7 of 7 resolved cases (network) and 5 of 5 (reference); magnitude predicted/observed median 1.10 (network, range 0.99-2.45), 1.13 (reference, range 1.03-1.67): over-predicted by 2.4x in X05 (-0.022 predicted, -0.009 observed), 1.3-1.7x in X09, 1.1-1.4x in X08. Delivered-heat difference: |error| <= 0.009 in all ten. The closed-form product rule eta x dphi_inc explains the thermal-storage cases (X04, X10) but not the others, as in development. Meaningful-routing classification 10 of 10 correct (X09 only); meaningful-incision classification 9 of 10 (X09 missed by the closed form).
* **Averaged forcing:** adequate (frozen rule: end share, delivered share within 0.05 and heat within 10 % of both sequences) in 8 of 10 test cases; **inadequate in X09 (end-share error 0.12-0.15) and X08 (heat)**. The averaging error was predicted by RTM_seq (X09 0.156/0.109 against 0.154/0.119 observed) and in X09 is **3.9 times larger than the order effect itself**. X09 is also the only configuration in which outlet 2 **dries out** during the low-discharge episode (13.3 h dry in AB, 11.8 h in BA, 0.8 h in the control, which stays wet at the mean discharge): with fixed geometry all three forcings end at the same share (0.273), whereas with incision the control cuts outlet 2 by 121 mm and the two sequences by 64-66 mm. The averaging failure there is an **activation (wet/dry) effect**, a non-smooth transition outside the validity of any smooth expansion, not a smooth nonlinearity.
* **Closure dependence (nu x0.5 / x2 on X04, X08, X09):** the sign of the share effect and the absence of a destination flip are unchanged; the magnitude changes by a factor 0.4-1.9 in X04 (0.010 / 0.024 / 0.046) and 0.54-1.3 in X09, but not in X08 (0.010 / 0.008 / 0.008); the heat difference moves less (0.10-0.18). The conclusion that destination is unchanged is closure-independent in these tests; the size of the routing effect is closure-dependent.
* **Tolerance:** the corrected energy tolerance (atol_energy 1e-3 J) reproduces the legacy share effect to four digits on X04 and X09.
* **Validity of the expansion:** the Lie-bracket formula reproduces the sequence difference of the reduced state (sign, tau^2 scaling, within 2-8 % at tau = 60 s) only for episodes shorter than about 5 minutes (spectral radius 1.4e-4 1/s); the 2-12 h episodes used here lie far outside, so the finite-episode predictions are exact RTM_seq compositions, and the bracket serves only to identify the mechanism terms (reservoir, thermal storage, geometry).

## Answers
**A. Destination or timing?** Not destination: no change in the dominant outlet in any configuration at any simulator (network, reference, closure variants). It changes the **partition** by up to 0.04 at the end and the cumulative delivered share by up to 0.07, the **heat delivered to the channels** by up to 18 %, and the first-crossing **switching time** by hours in near-tie cases; the persistent part after a common continuation is about 60-90 % of the partition effect in three configurations and negligible in the others.
**B. Mechanism.** Lake thermal storage (the lake temperature is a low-pass filter of the inlet temperature with flushing time V/Q) makes the heat delivered to the channels order-dependent although the incoming heat is equal; this changes incision, which shifts the partition by (eta) x (incision-induced share shift) to leading order; ordinary lake-level timing contributes only in X08 and X03. Wet/dry events are not needed for the order effect (nine of ten test cases keep both outlets wet after the first 3 h; minimum outlet-2 share 0.16-0.65), but the one case with outlet 2 drying out (X09) is the one with a routing-meaningful effect (delivered share -0.071) and the dominant averaging failure; in that case the order effect itself still follows the thermal-storage mechanism (route-1 incision 124 mm in AB, 111 mm in BA).
**C. Can the theory predict sign and magnitude outside development?** Sign: yes in all resolved cases (7/7, reference 5/5). Magnitude: within about 10-40 % in most cases, but with one 2.4-fold over-prediction (X05) and systematic over-prediction in X09; the heat difference is predicted to within 0.009. The prediction is made by RTM_seq, a reduction of the same kernels; the analytic pieces (eta, bracket terms) are partial.
**D. When is averaged forcing adequate?** When both outlets stay wet through the episodes and the thermal-storage contrast is moderate (X01-X07, X10: the frozen rule was met in 8 of 10; the averaging error is then of the size of the order effect or smaller). It fails when an outlet dries out in the sequences but not in the control (X09: end-share error 0.12-0.15, 3.9 times the order effect) or when the lake-temperature contrast makes the delivered heat differ by more than 10 % (X08, eta = 0.18); the averaging error is predicted by RTM_seq in both cases. Neither sign nor size of the order effect can be read from the averaged control.
**E. What is potentially original?** Only a specific bounded combination: matched water and discharge-weighted sensible-heat budgets with reversed order and a common continuation; the decomposition into reservoir, thermal-storage and geometry terms; the closed form for the heat delivered to the channels; and the (negative) finding that for two outlets of this type the order changes partition and incision energy but not destination. Time-varying flux in a single channel exists (Jarosch & Gudmundsson 2012, abstract only; full text unread), the history memory of a channel is stated by Schoof et al. (2023, sections 2 and 7), the Lie-bracket expansion and the averaging error of nonlinear laws are standard (fluvial runoff-variability literature, e.g. DiBiase & Whipple 2011, abstract only). Two relevant papers could not be read (hosts outside the session's network allowance); no priority claim is made.

## Failures, limits and unresolved items
Two 400-cell reference runs (X10) hit the 1200 s limit and one r33 run (X07) stalled with the legacy tolerance (F9); the near-boundary wrong-sign case of the previous milestone is unresolved and not re-tested (no test configuration lies within the capture-threshold zone with a resolved reference of the sign); RTM remains a reduction of the production kernels; heat-transfer closures are uncertain (magnitude changes by a factor up to 2); incision during the experiments is 0.3-1.5 of the head over the sill (translation-invariance stretched, as before); only 6 reference cases, two orders, two-route networks, 12-30 h horizons; the effect is bounded by 0.04 in end share in 20 configurations, so the possibility of a destination change in a region not sampled (very small lakes with a narrowly tied pair and long contrasting episodes) is not excluded.
## One justified next step
Reduce the closure uncertainty that now limits the size of the routing effect and of the incision effect: a resolved (turbulent, free-surface, melting-wall) heat-transfer benchmark for the open-channel Nusselt number in the regime of these channels (Re 1e4-1e5), or at minimum a published-experiment comparison, before any further sequence or capture study.
