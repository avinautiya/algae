# Forcing order, matched budgets and outlet selection: derivation and validity

Idealised two-outlet problem (lake of area A, two ice channels, temperate ice, shared Manning / Dittus-Boelter / critical-brink closures); no field data. RTM_seq is a reduction of the production kernels, not independent physics (carried over from the capture milestone).

## 1. State equations
Reduced state x = (V, T_L, d1, d2): lake volume, well-mixed lake temperature (relative to the melting point), effective sill lowering of each route (the rating-effective lowering E of `ice_network_capture/docs/capture_theory.md`). Forcing: inlet discharge Q(t) and temperature T(t). With H = z_b + V/A, u_i = H - s_i0 + d_i, ratings Q_i(u_i), tabulated rates E_i^(1)(u) at 1 C:

  dV/dt = Q - Q1(u1) - Q2(u2)                      (reservoir)
  dT_L/dt = Q (T - T_L)/V                          (thermal storage; exact for a well-mixed lake: V dT_L/dt = d(V T_L)/dt - T_L dV/dt)
  dd_i/dt = T_L E_i^(1)(u_i)                       (irreversible geometry; E_i is linear in T_L because every heat-transfer rate is, to the accuracy of the closure)

Accumulators (no feedback): delivered water C_i = integral Q_i dt, heat to the ice, sill lowering. A constant-forcing episode k defines a vector field f_k(x) = f(x; Q_k, T_k); an experiment is a composition of flows.

## 2. Budget matching
Episodes A, B, a common continuation and an averaged control. Water: integral Q dt. Sensible heat: rho_w c_w integral Q (T - T_m) dt. With T_m = 0, the control uses the **discharge-weighted** mean T_bar = integral Q T dt / integral Q dt (not the arithmetic mean); Q_bar = integral Q dt / duration. Smooth transitions are made by raised-cosine ramps in Q and in the heat flux Q T so that both budgets are exactly order-independent (verified to 1e-13 in `forcing.py`). The averaged control has the same budgets but not the same lake-temperature and geometry history. Equal incoming budgets do **not** imply equal heat delivered to the ice: lake storage differs.

## 3. Leading-order sequence difference
For smooth fields, Phi_B(Phi_A(x0)) - Phi_A(Phi_B(x0)) = tau_A tau_B [f_A, f_B](x0) + O(tau^3), [f_A, f_B] = Df_B f_A - Df_A f_B (Taylor expansion of both compositions; the common terms cancel). Components (derived, then verified numerically with central-difference Jacobians, `bracket.py`, `bracket_check.json`):
* reservoir: [ ]_V = -grad(Q_out) . (f_A - f_B) = (G/A)(Q_B - Q_A) + (geometry term dQ_out/dd . (f_d^B - f_d^A)), G = g1 + g2 the total rating sensitivity: an ordinary lag of the stage behind a different inflow;
* thermal storage: [ ]_T = Q_A Q_B (T_B - T_A)/V^2 - [Q_B (T_B - T_L) f_V^A - Q_A (T_A - T_L) f_V^B]/V^2, with f_V^k = Q_k - Q_out: a lake that has not flushed holds the temperature of the earlier episode;
* geometry (irreversible): [ ]_{d_i} = grad E_i . (f_A - f_B), including dE/dT_L (f_T^A - f_T^B): the lowering rate depends on the lake temperature which differs between orders.
For the accumulators (delivered water, heat to the ice) the leading difference AB - BA is tau_A tau_B grad(g) . (f_A - f_B) with g the integrand (not a bracket).
**Validity (measured):** with the 17-station tabulation of geometry A the Jacobians of both fields have spectral radius rho = 1.4e-4 1/s (lake-level mode, 2 h) and the other modes have time scales V/Q = 7-17 h (thermal) and 41 h (capture growth). The ratio exact/predicted is 1.01 (V), 0.98/0.92 (d1, d2) at tau = 60 s, and falls below 0.8 for the geometry components at tau rho = 0.04 (tau = 300 s) and below 0.5 at tau rho = 0.17; the sign of the geometry difference reverses at tau rho of about 0.34-0.68 (tau = 2400-4800 s). **The bracket is therefore valid only for episodes shorter than about 5 minutes here; the experiments use 2-12 h episodes (tau rho = 1-6), far outside it, and use the exact RTM_seq composition.** The expansion is used for the sign and structure (which term is present), not for the magnitude.
**Non-smooth events are excluded:** the expansion assumes u_1, u_2 > 0 throughout; an outlet activating or deactivating (u_i crossing 0), overtopping the lake sill or drying changes the vector field discontinuously and the composition difference is then governed by *when* the crossing occurs, not by a bracket. Among the ten test cases only X09 has an outlet drying out and re-wetting inside the experiment (identified from the stored share series: outlet-2 share < 0.5 % for 12-13 h in AB and BA); it is the case with the largest averaging error and the only routing-meaningful effect, and its prediction by RTM_seq (which includes the activation threshold through Q(u) = 0 for u <= 0) is not covered by the expansion.

## 4. Closed-form thermal-storage effect on the delivered heat
Quasi-steady lake level (outflow = inflow), constant V, heat to the ice at rate T_L kappa(Q) with kappa tabulated at the frozen-geometry partition. Over an episode of constant (Q, T) lasting tau: T_L(t) = T + (T_L0 - T) e^{-t/theta}, theta = V/Q, so the heat is kappa [T tau + (T_L0 - T) theta (1 - e^{-tau/theta})] and the lake temperature leaves the episode at T + (T_L0 - T) e^{-tau/theta}. Composition over AB, BA and the common continuation gives eta = (H_AB - H_BA)/mean (`theory_eta.py`). Limits: eta -> 0 for V -> 0 (instant mixing, T_L = T) and V -> infinity (T_L constant); eta is largest for tau of the order of the flushing time V/Q (the development cases with tau/theta = 0.6-1.2 have eta = 0.11-0.18; tau/theta = 4 gives |eta| < 0.02).
## 5. Routing consequence and dimensionless numbers
The share shift produced by incision is dphi_inc = phi(thermal) - phi(fixed geometry) for the same forcing. If the sequence only rescales the incision by (1 + eta/2) and (1 - eta/2), the share effect is delta phi ~ eta dphi_inc (product rule, tested in development: it captures the thermal-storage cases S01, S03, S07 within 0.003 and misses the geometry/hydraulic-driven ones S04, S05, S08, S10). Whether delta phi matters for routing is decided by comparing it with the distance of the share to a tie (|phi - 1/2|) and with the effect threshold fixed in `FREEZE.md`.
Dimensionless groups from the equations: epsilon_T = tau Q_bar/V (episode duration over flushing time; thermal storage effect needs epsilon_T of order 1), epsilon_L = tau G/A (episode over lake-level relaxation; reservoir timing needs epsilon_L of order 1 or smaller), epsilon_g = tau/tau_g with tau_g = 1/sigma (capture growth time, 41 h here), N_Q = (Q_B - Q_A)/Q_bar and N_T = (T_B - T_A)/T_bar (contrasts), Pi_inc = accumulated incision/head over the sill (0.25-1.5 in the tests, i.e. the translation-invariant reduction is stretched), and D_c = |gap - Delta_c|/Delta_c (distance from the capture threshold).
