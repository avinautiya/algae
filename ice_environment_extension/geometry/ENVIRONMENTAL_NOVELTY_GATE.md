# Environmental prediction gate

## One prediction worth testing

**Does history-dependent hydraulic contact change cumulative exposed-cliff-face
melt enough that a prescribed-contact model gives the wrong response to debris
clearing, across defensible obstruction transmission and geometry ranges?**

The candidate result is not that streams maintain cliffs, that cliffs retreat,
or that debris shades ice. It is a quantified, falsifiable *conditional error
in a predictive approximation*: treating contact as restored immediately after
clearing versus allowing an incised bypass to keep water away from the face.
An impactful outcome would identify when that approximation changes a physical
prediction beyond combined numerical and closure uncertainty. This is a local
model-comparison claim; field predictive superiority and historical priority
remain separate gates.

## Existing knowledge and priority boundary

- Buri et al. (2016, DOI 10.1002/2016JF004039) already model evolving cliff
  geometry and energy balance. A moving cliff or an energy-budget model is not
  a first contribution.
- Buri and Pellicciotti (2018) already address aspect-dependent cliff evolution
  and persistence. Do not claim that insolation changes cliff survival is new.
- Evatt et al. (2024, DOI 10.1017/aog.2024.34) revisit cliff dynamics and the
  surrounding surface-lowering frame. Fixed-crest patch calculations cannot
  replace that treatment.
- Petersen et al. (2024, DOI 10.5194/esurf-12-727-2024) already observe stream
  maintenance, abandonment, debris removal and reburial; relative debris
  evacuation and thermoerosional effects remain unresolved in their account.
- Kneib et al. (2022, DOI 10.5194/tc-16-4701-2022) belongs in the comparison of
  cliff evolution and debris evacuation. The exact coupled contact operator
  must be checked in its full methods before making an omission/priority claim.
- Kraaijenbrink and Immerzeel (2025, DOI 10.1029/2024JF007935;
  Zenodo 13073856) supply repeated Himalayan cliff profiles. This establishes
  available observed geometry, not an omission in all earlier models.
- Sato et al. (2025, DOI 10.1017/jog.2025.17) also model seasonal cliff
  evolution with surface energy balance. This is a different study from the
  Kraaijenbrink dataset. Its contact treatment must be read at equation level
  before making a priority claim.

These sources establish substantial prior art. The gate below can establish a
new result *within this study*; it does not by itself establish first discovery.

## Bounded decisive comparison

Freeze forcing, geometry, transmission values, clearing times, output support
and acceptance rules **before evaluation**. Do not select cases for large melt
differences after viewing outputs.

1. **Common physical support.** Register one extruded cliff-face patch, floor
   patch and width. Face and floor removals must be disjoint. Report face melt,
   floor melt and their sum separately; do not compare different ice footprints.
2. **Matched models.** Use identical atmosphere, cold content, debris storage,
   geometry, transmission closure and heat-transfer coefficients. Change only
   dynamic hydraulic contact versus prescribed contact after clearing. Include
   a fixed pre-clearing-contact baseline as a bracket, not a selected winner.
3. **Paired interventions.** No obstruction; obstruction without removal;
   early removal; late removal, each across declared porous transmission
   and initial face/floor geometry ranges. Maintain paired common forcing.
4. **Primary endpoint.** Cumulative **face** ice melt volume in m3 over a fixed
   post-clearing horizon, and the paired difference between contact models.
   Also report absolute error against the independently resolved model, when
   one represents the same geometry/closures, not just disagreement between
   two reduced models.
5. **Secondary endpoints.** Integrated bare exposed face area-time in m2 s;
   integrated bare wet face area-time; water-to-face latent energy; floor melt;
   time to contact loss/reconnection; duration of low contact; final geometry.
   Separate maintained contact, genuine reconnection and failed reconnection.
   Shared pre-removal trajectories are one loss event, not independent repeats.
6. **Uncertainty.** Refine mesh, timestep and geometry/contact coupling near the
   recovery boundary. Report conditional numerical error separately from
   closure/forcing spread. Compare different splitting orders when face retreat
   and floor lowering act together. A closed ledger is necessary, not sufficient.

The current uniform-planar face operator can report integrated face melt but
cannot predict undercutting or shape changes from heterogeneous face fluxes.
Its exposed-area diagnostic assumes prescribed debris and water elevations.
Any campaign using it must preserve those labels. Adding flux components does
not silently create a validated collapse or debris-transport model.

## Nulls and falsifiers

- **Primary null:** dynamic contact does not improve independently benchmarked
  face-melt predictions beyond the declared uncertainty over prescribed contact.
  A large difference between models alone does not reject this null.
- **Mechanism falsifier:** recovery disappears when realistic transmission is
  admitted, or persists only for an impermeable strip/invalid hydrostatic regime.
  Then the strong environmental mechanism is unsupported.
- **Practical falsifier:** face-melt differences are smaller than uncertainty
  from atmosphere, debris, cold content, heat transfer or geometry. Then the
  contact refinement has not established useful melt-prediction impact.
- **Robustness falsifier:** sign or classification changes under converged
  grid/coupling refinements or equivalent patch support. Resolve that before
  producing a headline environmental conclusion.
- **Geometry falsifier:** the effect relies on uniform face retreat where
  differential water/air/debris melting would control shape/contact. Then a
  resolved moving-face treatment is required before environmental inference.

## What the 83 profile epochs can validate

These are **83 profile epochs**, not 83 independent cliffs or 83 independent
forecast tests. Retain glacier, cliff, profile and survey-date grouping. The
point cloud bins describe corrected geometry along a transect; binning spread
and cross-profile width are not independent measurement uncertainties.

They can test data ingestion, units, geometry extraction, observed slope/height
and change ranges, and whether an ideal vertical-step geometry is remotely
representative. With explicitly matched repeated profiles and survey errors,
they may evaluate profile change or constrain plausible geometry ensembles.

They cannot validate stream discharge/temperature, water contact, debris
transmission, clearing events, debris cover or the energy partition without
additional colocated observations. A profile lacks out-of-plane width, so it
does not independently give whole-cliff melt volume. Surface lowering can
include motion, collapse and geometry changes beyond local thermal melt.
Do not fit hydraulic parameters to those changes and call the same profiles
independent validation. Hold out whole cliffs or glacier groups if model fitting
is later attempted; a random split of points/epochs leaks shared geometry.

## Pass interpretation

A pass supports a controlled computational result: a specified contact
approximation produces a consequential face-melt error in a stated range.
Environmental transfer requires realistic forcing and support for the contact,
transmission and face-evolution closures. Neither this gate nor a favorable
title establishes universal superiority over glacier models.
