# Prior art and the remaining inner-seal problem

Focused audit on 31 October 2026. The candidate is a **stateful, nonuniform inner-seal model derived consistently on the fast timescale**, intended to predict breach/reseal transitions. Neither the full problem nor historical originality is established by the executable linearized prototype. A semigroup representation, Volterra kernel, dynamic rating or history-dependent discharge is not independently a new mathematical framework.

## Closest documented gap

[Schoof, Cook, Kulessa & Thompson (2023), *The drainage of glacier and ice sheet surface lakes*](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2023.130), [full text](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-fluid-mechanics/article/drainage-of-glacier-and-ice-sheet-surface-lakes/6A699229F6F8C928BA52E83ABFEC16B7). **Scope:** model/scaling in sections 2.1–2.2, seal/shock analysis in 3.3–3.5, oscillations/discussion in 6–7, and Appendix B inspected. Moving seals, multiple flux branches, backward knickpoints and repeated drainage are already results. Section 3.5 explicitly identifies an inconsistency: its fast regularization uses a steady inner flux relation, although the fast inner bed equation acquires a time derivative. The authors leave that analysis open. Their original St Venant continuity already includes wall-melt mass and the ice/water density ratio; the reduced constant-flux approximation follows a small-parameter ordering. The defensible target is a consistent fast inner relation and transition criterion, not the first moving seal or first melt source.

## What remains to be solved

The linearized finite-reach prototype retains a nonuniform seal base and isolates a state-elimination calculation. It does not yet establish the infinite-pond match to both outer solutions or derive nonlinear branch selection. It is restricted to hydraulic subcriticality. The resulting convolution is standard linear-systems mathematics. Its role is verification and exploration of model structure.

A substantive next mathematical result would derive the inner evolution and matching from a declared ordering, show recovery of the slow relation where valid, and identify when history changes the selected breach/reseal trajectory. Thermal state should enter only if its relaxation time survives that ordering. The mass source, low-momentum melt injection, moving-bed storage, density jump and hydraulic pressure terms require consistent control-volume accounting. A different term retained in isolation is not automatically a new model.

The useful forecast target is transition timing and the resulting outflow, interruption or retained water. Two histories with matched reduced observables but different predictions would demonstrate insufficiency of that reduction; this alone would not prove new physics or historical priority. Agreement with a simulator embedding the same closure is verification, not independent environmental validation.

## Follow-up search coverage

Searches used the exact title/DOI and all four author names, unsteady-seal and boundary-layer terms, kinetic relations, critical meltwater controls and 2023–2026 follow-ups. No resolution of the specific fast-inner issue was located in the inspected results. Cambridge's citation display returned no CrossRef data and its Scholar link could not be fetched. Full forward-citation coverage was therefore not obtained. An open step stated in a 2023 paper is a credible research target; this audit does not certify that it remains unpublished today.

A final targeted screen used glacier lake seal/drainage with algebraic memory, memory-kernel and Volterra terms. It located established conduit/reservoir stability literature but no verified prior statement of the exact canonical tail bound derived here. This keyword screen does not certify priority; algebraic transport memory and positive-kernel methods are broadly established.

## Decision and hard falsifiers

Proceed as a **candidate model-development problem**, not a solved open problem or a Nature-level novelty claim. The central contribution fails if the consistent inner dynamics reduce to the existing rating within relevant error, if the effect is ordinary fixed-geometry hydraulic hysteresis, or if it appears only at unresolved criticality. Thermal novelty fails if removing thermal delay changes no resolved transition. Historical novelty fails if close work already derives the same matching and selection law. A robust forecast advantage must include existing coupled and dynamic-rating comparators, not only a static or fixed-bed baseline.
