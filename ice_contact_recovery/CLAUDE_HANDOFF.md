# Bounded handoff for Claude

This packet is additive. Do not replace your current solver, restart jobs or
repeat campaigns merely to incorporate it. Complete your current verification
milestone first. Earlier frozen outputs and failures remain part of the record.

## 1. Apply immediate low-cost checks

- Run the two test suites and thermal-clock script from README. Transfer the
  thermal-clock null to your solver ONLY in its applicable regime: constant Q,
  fixed debris, steady homogeneous passive heat, no real-time extra forcing.
  Compare matched thermal doses with exact event times and morphology-step
  refinement. Atmospheric flux, storage, frictional heat and material motion
  invalidate that null unless disabled for this diagnostic.
- Preserve the previous package's exact removal scheduling and explicit actual
  removal timestamps. Recovery is assessed after return to ordinary discharge,
  never from flood-only wetting.
- If post-abandonment bed arrays are available, evaluate the spatial energy
  bound over a range of defensible **upper** local water levels. Use the width
  relaxation by default; full-cell graph costs are not continuum bounds.

## 2. Strongest next prediction

The primary question is whether post-clearance ice geometry makes debris export
an unreliable predictor of restored basal heat delivery. For one existing
recovering and one nonrecovering case, compare the same complete clearing with:

1. full moving-ice history;
2. differential ice incision disabled during abandonment;
3. exactly the same post-abandonment ice bed, restarted without debris.

These controls distinguish remaining blockage from substrate memory. Preserve
porous/lateral transmission and physically appropriate entrance/outlet geometry.
No impermeable deposit or fixed sill may be introduced merely to force failure.
Use sustained ordinary-flow toe delivery and integrated **local** ice heat as
the outcomes, with numerical and closure uncertainty. A plan-view floor model
does not by itself predict the life of a full vertical ice cliff.

## 3. Use a meaningful forecasting baseline

The new scalar water-budget tool is a theorem-backed baseline for first contact.
It accounts for pond filling AND bypass leakage. Extract its area and discharge
curve from independent geometry/verified hydraulics, not by fitting each test
outcome. Test peak-only, storage-only and leakage-aware predictors separately.
Its static-toe necessary bounds do not apply if the toe appreciably lowers
during filling unless a separately justified optimistic target is used. Include
meltwater and all other water sources in the mass budget.

An environmental contribution is strongest if a geometry-aware predictor
improves a relevant held-out forecast. Beating a deliberately static or
debris-only baseline is a causal comparison, not proof of superiority to all
existing glacier models.

## 4. Optional physical-order test, gated on a non-negligible signal

Read `physical.md` and the independent order diagnostic if included. First check
the smooth regime away from wet/dry thresholds. Compare equal-joint Q,T forcing
cycles, return to identical ordinary flow, and compute the small-duration
commutator prediction independently. If the response is tiny relative to
numerical/closure uncertainty, stop this branch of investigation. Do not launch
a large cycle campaign just because a classical bracket is nonzero.

## 5. Report evidence precisely

Distinguish proved conditional mathematics, independently checked synthetic
examples, verified coupled-model predictions and external validation. The
candidate specific novelty is the quantitative post-clearance recovery boundary,
not generic ice/debris coupling or history dependence. Primary sources already
contain those broader mechanisms. State search/reading limits from `prior_art.md`.

Keep all comparisons and failed predictions. Generate final figures from saved
records: recovery boundary, contact hydrographs and post-flood persistence,
bed/route evolution, local heat delivery, forecast error, conservation and
resolution/closure sensitivity. New synthetic figures in this packet are
method checks, not final environmental results.
