# Scientific decision before more computation

## Status

The mathematics in THEOREMS.md establishes conditional model results. It supplies a more consequential candidate observable than ordinary entrance-region thermal development: the sign and size of a delayed response of retained lake water to an added outlet.

**Nature-level historical novelty is not established. Environmental predictive superiority is not established.** A deadline does not convert those unknowns into evidence. This packet makes no production request on that premise.

## What can be said now

- Under the stated stable drainage assumptions, a higher non-eroding outlet can initially increase drainage while increasing the water remaining after the episode. Proposition 1 gives the exact nonlinear retention identity; Proposition 2 covers variable lake area for proportional incision.
- In a soluble class, the sign-reversal time is known exactly. The predicted effect survives an open stable parameter range; it is not an effect that exists only at an exactly tuned balance.
- A fixed-bed monotone reservoir necessarily misses the sign of that final counterfactual response. The exact formula is analytically superior to that restricted baseline for this model-class observable.
- Existing coupled drainage models already contain the required feedback or a related architecture. Superiority over those models has not been established and is not implied.
- The original 120-case heat result remains unchanged: the classical two-mode model has the best demonstrated average error. A theory about another target does not overturn it.

## What would make this a scientific contribution

The strongest defensible hypothesis is that the retention and delayed-response criterion identifies a practically important regime that existing studies have not characterized, and provides a useful forecast or control diagnostic that can be extracted from more resolved models.

The mathematical identity alone is insufficient to establish that significance. The required distinctions are:

1. **Priority:** whether the exact intervention response/criterion is already implicit *and explicitly analyzed* in the closest studies. Finding the equations in prior work weakens an omitted-physics claim even if the exact formula was not printed there.
2. **Physical regime:** whether realistic heat capture, control geometry, hydraulic ratings and finite forcing episodes admit the required ratios without violating their energy supply or changing the hydraulic control.
3. **Independent model consequence:** whether resolved physics preserves a forecast-relevant effect and whether a strong existing coupled baseline already captures it. Agreement between a formula and a simulator embedding the same closure verifies implementation, not a new environmental law.
4. **Forecast information:** whether parameters and forcing are known at the forecast origin. Future measured pulse summaries cannot silently be used in a predictive claim.
5. **Relevance:** whether the actual use is drainage duration, retained volume, peak release, or local incision. None alone establishes improved glacier mass-balance prediction, Himalayan transfer, satellite correction or GLOF hazard skill.

This is a criterion for evaluating the candidate, not a new request to launch tests. No expensive campaign is warranted solely because the formulas look complex.

It is more reviewable than a promise to add a PINN or more physics until a metric improves. It remains a theory candidate, not an established Nature discovery, and no award outcome can be inferred.
