## Candidate distinction

The narrow candidate contribution is a finite-bypass retention identity and its hypsometric extension, plus an exact finite-time crossing criterion and an explicit forecast consequence. None of the inspected passages states that exact combination. This is a search outcome, not proof that no prior paper contains it.

The result is a consequence of an existing class of coupled mass/incision equations. If a prior model already predicts it, the possible contribution becomes an analytical characterization and application. That can be publishable but cannot be advertised as correcting physics absent from every professional model.

## Directions deliberately not promoted as new

- [Bubble-enhanced glacier melting, Nature Geoscience 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41561-023-01262-8): bubble-driven mixing and enhanced melting have already been studied; adding bubbles alone is not original.
- [Density-anomaly effects on ice melting, primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09879): fresh-water buoyancy and melting across the density maximum are established subjects.
- [Geometric heat pumping, primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19100): cyclic modulation and geometric contributions to heat transport already have theory and experiments; a generic “geometric heat pump” claim would overreach.
- [Jarosch & Gudmundsson 2012](https://tc.copernicus.org/articles/6/493/2012/): coupling channel hydraulics, heat transfer, incision and ice dynamics is established. More coupled equations are not, by themselves, a new discovery.

These last entries are a screening record, not a comprehensive review of their subfields. They explain why the packet pursues a specific provable counterfactual rather than claiming that adding one familiar mechanism guarantees novelty.

## Unresolved priority checks

Full Raymond–Nolan equations and examples; transient circuit responses in Clarke; the underlying SRLF/Raymond–Nolan implementation and its treatment of competing drainage; spillway intervention/siphon reconstructions cited by Ogier; and cross-field erosion/reservoir counterfactuals remain relevant. No claim of “all literature examined” or “first ever” is authorized by this audit.
