# Prior-art comparison for the proposed inversion (milestone 1)

Accessed 2026-10-30. Machine-readable version: `records/inversion_m1/prior_art.csv` (one row per method, with source URL, verification level and the local hash of what was read).

**Proposed research question (a hypothesis, not a novelty claim).** Can a chemistry-constrained optical inversion distinguish algal and mineral darkening on previously unseen glaciers more reliably than existing fixed-optics methods, and does that improve independent radiative predictions?

## Verification levels (used throughout)

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **FT** | Full text read, or source code inspected, in this session. |
| **AB** | Publisher or repository abstract and metadata read (Crossref, DataCite, a repository record). Full text not read. |
| **SS** | Only a web-search summary of the page. Treated as a lead, not as evidence. |

Several pages were unreachable from this environment (HTTP 403 from AGU/Wiley, ScienceDirect, science.org, GFZ and the GitHub web UI; PubMed did not resolve). Git clones from GitHub worked. Where a paper is marked AB or SS, its methods have **not** been checked here.

## Himalayan relevance

No Himalayan observation of algae and mineral dust measured together on ice or snow (spectra plus composition) was found in this search, and the repository has none. What was verified at metadata/abstract level:

- Takeuchi 2002, *Annals of Glaciology* (doi:10.3189/172756402781817743): laboratory visible-reflectance of cryoconite from nine glaciers in the Himalaya, Tibet and the Arctic, with the albedo differences related to mineral and organic content.
- Pan et al. 2026, *Polar Science* (doi:10.1016/j.polar.2026.101437): dust-induced bare-ice albedo reduction on Parlung No. 4 Glacier, southeast Tibetan Plateau (metadata only; not in the Himalaya proper).
- Di Mauro et al. 2020, *Scientific Reports* (doi:10.1038/s41598-020-61762-0): glacier algae and albedo in the European Alps (abstract only).

Greenland algae optics and Greenland dust optics cannot be assumed to transfer to Himalayan glaciers. Any Himalayan statement needs Himalayan observations that the repository does not contain.

## What existing methods already do (do not relabel as new)

1. Separating algal, mineral and black-carbon darkening from spectral albedo with a radiative-transfer emulator and an inversion: **snowlaps** (snow).
2. Optimal-estimation retrieval of algae on glacier ice with posterior uncertainty and error correlations from imaging spectroscopy: **Bohn et al. 2022** (one scene, no dust/BC state, no validation).
3. A Bayesian, uncertainty-aware inversion on Greenland bare ice with empirical algae, cryoconite and dust optics, and an empirically constrained ice-column model: **Chevrollier et al. 2026**, at abstract level only. **If its full text confirms this, the central separation capability already exists for the Dark Zone and our contribution must be defined against it.**
4. Libraries of measured or empirical particle optics, with selectable alternatives for dust: **SNICAR-fx**, **Chevrollier 2023**.
5. Emulator-in-the-loop inversion in general. A PINN in the strict sense (physics residuals inside the loss) was not found for this problem; the closest published approach is a data-trained emulator of a physical model, which is **not** a PINN.

## The exact remaining capability the proposal could add (conditional)

Not demonstrated by any source read here, and unverified against the 2026 paper:

- **R1.** Treating the optical properties of the absorbers (algal pigment optics and mineral/cryoconite optics) as a small number of *inferred* low-dimensional degrees of freedom, constrained by measured spectral libraries, rather than as one chosen fixed set, and reporting when abundance and optical variation cannot be separated.
- **R2.** Evaluating that inversion under leave-glacier-out splits against held-out spectra and independent component measurements, with the matched A/B/C ablation that separates chemical variability from inference algorithm.

Both depend on (i) reading the Dark Zone paper's full methods and (ii) observations that exist. Section "Narrowing" in `docs/inversion_m1/limitations.md` records what the available observations allow.

## Blocking dependency for any novelty statement

The full text and inversion code of Chevrollier et al. 2026 could not be read here. Before any claim of a differentiated capability is made, someone with access must read: how the optics are chosen or varied in the inversion, which glaciers or sites validate it, whether validation is spectral, albedo or melt, and whether uncertainty includes optical-library choice. This item is also listed as the blocking dependency in the milestone verdict.
