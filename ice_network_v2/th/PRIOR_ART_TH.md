Established by Jones/classical theory (and therefore **not** a contribution here): for Poiseuille flow between plates the downstream temperature field is determined by the inlet temperature *profile* through the Graetz eigenfunction coefficients, not by its mean; after a sudden wall-temperature change the wall flux is singular like x^(−1/3) (Lévêque region) and relaxes to the fully developed Nusselt number over a length ~ H·Pe; axial conduction matters only within O(Pe^−1/3·H) of the discontinuity (Jones). That two flows with equal bulk temperature but different thermal development give different wall heat flux is therefore a textbook consequence of the Graetz problem. The same experiment in the straight control reproduces it (δ ≈ 20 % over 10 H) and the resolved upstream development agrees with the Graetz series to 0.03–0.10 % in T_b and 0.26–0.28 % in wall flux (analysis.json `graetz_check`).
Not established by the sources read: the *size* of the effect downstream of a sharp bed step, relative to the straight development, its numerical uncertainty, and its consequence for a reach-scale conductance forecast. That quantified, geometry-specific bookkeeping is the only candidate content of this milestone; it is an application of known theory and is not described as new physics.
