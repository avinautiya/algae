## Reference error, corrected
The estimate of the discretisation error of the 400- and 800-cell references now uses the measured orders of the 100/200/400/800 sequence (`reference_uncertainty.py`): supported (0.6-1.06) for hydrograph, incision, delivered volume and t_dom2, **not supported for t_act2**; e_400 = d(400->800)/(1 - 2^-p), e_800 = d(400->800)/(2^p - 1). The earlier use of |400 - 800| and a hypothetical order 0.5 is superseded (`CONVERGENCE_TABLE.md`, `FROZEN_S1_S5_ANALYSIS.md`, `records/REPORT_TABLES.md` updated). ## Verdict 1 - accuracy-cost advantage at matched accuracy: **not demonstrated**
Frozen matching rule (serial repeats on an idle single worker, max/min of three repeats 1.01-1.06, so timings are reproducible and not a concurrency or JIT artefact: warm-up 0.2-0.3 s): r9 and r17 are *not matched* (their hydrograph errors 0.021-0.050 exceed the 100-cell reference errors 0.020-0.027 of the same scenario), so no speed-up is claimed for them; r17 is 1.8-2.0 times cheaper than the 100-cell reference (10.2 against 18.3 s in S1, 10.2 against 20.7 s in S2, 6.1 against 17.6 s in S5) at an 8-15 % larger hydrograph error (S2, S5, S1). r33 is matched, with speed-ups of **0.68 (S1), 0.31 (S2) and 2.37 (S5)**: slower than the reference in two of three scenarios (S2 r33 takes 153 s serially, against 56 s for the 200-cell reference). The cost of r33 comes from the discharge-law solve (Brent iterations 4.9 -> 7.6, 0.33 -> 0.87 ms per iteration), not from the geometry update. No optimisation was made.
## Verdict 2 - robustness of routing to physical assumptions: **destination robust only where the outlets are not nearly tied; switching time and incision magnitude are not robust**
(Reference with closure applied to both models; `records/closure_summary.json`.) Of 20 closure variants (heat-transfer x0.5 and x2; Manning 0.03 and 0.05; S1-S5) the destination changed in 1 (S1, near-tie, 5.6 % margin, heat transfer x2: R1 -> R2). Whether R2 ever overtakes R1 changed in 4 (S1 x0.5 and n = 0.03: lost; S4 and S5 with n = 0.05: appears) and the switching time moved by -36 % to +30 % in 6 (S1 n = 0.05 -8.9 ks, S1 x2 -8.4 ks, S2 +349 s, -562 s, +2 367 s, -729 s). Activation time moved by at most about 2 %. Incision scales linearly with the heat-transfer multiplier (x0.5 -> 0.49-0.50; x2 -> 1.96-2.10), so a factor-2 closure uncertainty is a factor-2 incision uncertainty, larger than the numerical error. Agreement between the two models under shared closures says nothing about the closures themselves.
## Verdict 3 - difference from the existing-method baseline: **large for incision and for melt-driven switching; none for outlet choice** (`records/baseline_summary.json`)
Baselines are dissipation-only incision (T2, SaDS-type), the same with fixed aspect (T3) and no melting (T0), inside our own hydraulics, evaluated against the thermal reference. They are surrogates, **not** SaDS or SHED (which were not run). With 1 C inflow the dissipation-only baselines produce 0.1-0.5 mm of sill lowering against 14-62 mm in the reference (99-100 % lower). Outlet choice is identical to the reference in all five scenarios for every baseline. In S1 the reference switches at 23.4 ks and both thermal configurations switch (r17 16.6 ks, r33 21.6 ks) while every baseline loses the switch; in S2 the switch is hydraulic: baselines switch at 6.4 ks (r17) and 8.5 ks (r33) against 7.8 ks (reference) and the thermal network 6.0 / 7.6 ks, i.e. no advantage for the thermal model. Hydrograph error (r17, vs 400-cell reference): thermal 0.029 / 0.029 / 0.019 / 0.024 / 0.021 (S1-S5), baselines 0.048 / 0.057 / 0.036 / 0.016 / 0.021; the baseline is worse than the thermal network by 0.01-0.03 in S1-S3 (larger than the r33 numerical error of 0.008-0.011), but **better in S4** and equal in S5. The fixed-terrain resolved reference (no melting) is 0.0065-0.079 of the inflow away from the thermal reference (0.0065 in S5, 0.024 in S4, 0.049-0.079 in S1-S3). So the thermal coupling matters for incision magnitude and, in marginal scenarios, for switching, and not for where the water goes.
## Verdict 4 - labels: **"adaptive" and "bio-inspired" are not warranted**
Implemented: (1) redistribution through fixed connections and (2) activation of predefined connections by stage, with melt-driven change of channel geometry; **not** (3) creation, relocation or removal of pathways (`Network.edges` is fixed at construction and was audited in every run: no connection was ever created). "Adaptive" would be accurate only for geometry adaptation of a fixed graph; the term "adaptive network" is misleading and should be replaced by "thermally evolving channel network on a fixed candidate graph". "Bio-inspired" contributes no necessary mathematical structure: the evolution law is the physical melt law (advected sensible heat, wetted-perimeter partition, latent heat), with no conductivity-reinforcement, growth or pruning rule (`docs/prior_art_comparison.md`); the label should be removed. No topology machinery was added because the SaDS/SHED review and the results above identify the differentiated capability as advected-heat incision, which does not need it, and because testing paths a graph cannot represent needs a 2-D resolved reference that does not exist here.

## Novelty verdict
**Not established.** Supported by this evaluation: a conservative reduced model with an independent resolved reference, a measured accuracy/cost relation, and a demonstration (within our hydraulics) that advected sensible heat dominates incision by about two orders of magnitude over dissipation-only closures at 1 C inflow and decides one melt-driven switch (S1). Not supported: a cost advantage; any physical validation (no observations; the closures are unvalidated; the laminar Graetz benchmark verifies a laminar configuration only); novelty relative to the literature (SaDS dissipation-only incision and SHED friction-only incision were read; other thermally driven channel-evolution models were not reviewed in this milestone, so no priority claim is made).

## Remaining limitations (not addressed here)
Two-route networks only; 8 h horizon; r17 over-predicts R2 incision by 13.9-20.7 % and R1 by about 2 % (resolution-limited for R2, unexplained residual for R1); a 1 mm activation floor is a numerical regularisation; stalls still occurred in 2 of 32 r9 challenge runs near first overtopping and one LSODA failure in r17; low-inflow cases (about 0.01 m3/s) contain all five unexplained failures; the quasi-steady, subcritical and storage-neglect envelope did not predict failure; challenge conclusions are limited to the sampled space (32 points) and 7 of the challenge points are degenerate.
## One justified next step (not started)
A new, independent assessment after a single targeted change: non-uniform station placement near the sill and crest (the dominant error is station-limited and station cost grows steeply) tested at equal station count and wall time against the uniform r17, on newly drawn development and evaluation points. Nothing further was run.
