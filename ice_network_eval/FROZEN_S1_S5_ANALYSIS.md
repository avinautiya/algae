# Frozen S1-S5 evaluation: all 35 configurations (published analysis)

Idealised controlled ice-water problems (`competing_outlets_v1.json`, sha256 in `.sha256`, unchanged). No real-glacier claim. Every number below is generated from immutable per-run records (`records/runs/*/record.json`, `series.npz`) by `analyze_campaign.py`, `closure_analysis.py` and `make_report.py`; full tables: `records/REPORT_TABLES.md`; figures: `figures/hydro_S1.png`, `hydro_S2.png`, `events.png`, `accuracy_cost_frozen.png`, `route_change_S2.gif`.

Expected 35, completed 35, failed 0, timed out 0, stalled 0, interrupted 0, pending 0. Per scenario: reference 100/200/400 cells and reduced r5/dt1800, r9/dt900, r17/dt450, r33/dt225. ## 1. Reduced-versus-reference numerical error under shared closures
Both models use Manning n 0.04, k_e 0.5, Dittus-Boelter with the same wall partition, critical-depth brink and the same terrain function; agreement therefore tests numerics and the reduced-order representation, not the closures.
* **Reference error first (corrected estimate).** With four meshes (100/200/400/800, S1-S4) the observed orders are supported for the hydrograph (0.93-0.98), incision (R1 about 1.0, R2 0.72-0.91), delivered volume (0.6-0.92) and t_dom2 (S1 1.06, S2 0.46-0.73); for t_act2 the order is not supported (differences of 1-5 s, non-monotone). The estimated discretisation error of the **400-cell target** is e_400 = d(400->800)/(1 - 2^-p): hydrograph RMSE/I 0.0070-0.0097 (S1-S4), R2 incision 0.12-0.64 mm, t_dom2 1 310 s (S1) and 99 s (S2); the S5 value assumes order 1 (three meshes only). Earlier text used the 200->400 change for this; it was close for the hydrograph but is replaced by these estimates, tabulated in `records/REPORT_TABLES.md` and `records/reference_uncertainty.json`. These are extrapolation estimates, not bounds. Reduced errors of the size of e_400 (r33 hydrograph 0.0069-0.0108) are not distinguishable from reference error.
* **Route selection.** Reduced r17 and r33 select the reference route in all five scenarios (S1 R1, S2 R2, S3-S5 R1). r5 and r9 select the wrong route in S1 (a near-tie: delivered 546 against 489 m3, margin 5.6 %).
* **Switching time (t_dom2: first time Q_R2 > Q_R1).** Occurs in the 400-cell reference only in S1 (23 443 s) and S2 (7 775 s). Reduced errors: S1 r17 -6 807 s, r33 -1 862 s, r5 -10 854 s, r9 -9 978 s; S2 r17 -1 778 s, r33 -136 s, r5 -2 402 s, r9 -2 407 s (all early). In S4 and S5 r5/r9 produce a switch the reference does not. Activation of R2 (t_act2): r5/r9/r17 are 185-310 s early in every scenario, r33 within 0.8-8.2 s.
* **Hydrograph error (RMSE of both outlets / inflow, vs 400 cells).** r5 0.040-0.057, r9 0.034-0.050, r17 0.019-0.029, r33 0.0069-0.0108. Cumulative delivered-volume error (relative to total): r17 0.028-0.044, r33 0.012-0.016.
* **Incision error (sill lowering vs 400 cells).** R1: within -1.0 to +1.3 mm of 13.7-52 mm; R2: +2.0 to +12.3 mm of 15.7-61 mm (r17 +3.2 to +9.5 mm, r33 +2.0 to +6.1 mm), i.e. an over-prediction of R2 incision by **9.4-26.4 %** (S1-S4). Definition: R2 = the steeper 60 m route; quantity = lowering of its sill after 8 h; error = reduced minus 400-cell reference; denominator = the 400-cell reference lowering of R2 (54.9, 60.2, 61.5, 15.7 mm in S1-S4; S5 excluded because its reference lowering is ~0). By configuration (all 16 S1-S4 cases): r33 9.4-12.8 %, r17 13.9-20.7 %, r9 17.6-26.2 %, r5 18.4-26.4 %. Against the 800-cell target of the convergence study the baseline r17 error is 13.5-20.3 %. It decreases with station refinement (to 3.5-4.5 % at 65 stations) and is independent of geometry step, substeps and rtol: see `CONVERGENCE_TABLE.md`. S5 incision is ~0 in both models.
* **Conservation (bookkeeping, not accuracy).** Water and energy residuals 1e-15 to 3e-15 relative; geometry-versus-latent-heat ice inventory 1e-7 to 1.2e-4 relative (S5: 3e-2 relative but 1.2e-9 kg absolute, a ratio of two near-zero quantities).
* **Cost and accuracy-cost (two concurrent workers on four cores; serial timing is the separate section below).** At a hydrograph error of about 2-3 % r17 takes 6.4-10.7 s against 19.4-24.3 s for the 100-cell reference, whose error is 0.0195-0.0269 (r17 error is 0.019-0.029): about 2-3x cheaper at comparable error. At about 1 % error r33 (20-154 s) against the 200-cell reference (51-64 s, error 0.0065-0.0092): no saving in S1-S4 (up to 2.7x slower in S2), 2.5x cheaper only in S5. r5 and r9 are not on the efficient frontier.
* **Applicability diagnostics** (S1 reference case): r_qs max 0.039, channel storage / node storage 0.28 %, melt/flow 0.94 %, Re 2.3e4-1.7e5 (above the nominal pipe-correlation range). Per-run diagnostics exist for runs started after they were added to the runner (all closure, convergence, parameter and challenge runs).

## 2. Sensitivity to physical closures (closure applied identically to both models; reference 200 cells)
* **Matched-closure numerical error** (r17 hydrograph RMSE/I) stays 0.021-0.044 across all variants; it is independent of the closure at the level of this study.
* **Incision magnitude is linear in the heat-transfer multiplier**: x0.5 -> 0.49-0.50 of base, x2 -> 1.96-2.10; Manning 0.03 -> 1.13-1.23 and 0.05 -> 0.85-0.90 (S1-S4). A factor-2 closure uncertainty is a factor-2 incision uncertainty, larger than the 9-26 % numerical bias in R2 incision (definition in section 1) and, unlike it, not reducible by refinement.
* **Activation time is robust**: t_act2 shifts by at most about 80 s (~2 %).
* **Switching time and destination are not robust in marginal cases.** S1 (near-tie): the destination changes from R1 to R2 with heat transfer x2; the switching event disappears with heat transfer x0.5 and with Manning 0.03, and moves from 24.9 ks to 16.0 ks with Manning 0.05. S2: t_dom2 shifts +349 s (nu x0.5), -562 s (x2), +2 367 s (+30 %, Manning 0.03), -729 s (Manning 0.05); destination R2 unchanged. S4 and S5 develop a late switch (19.95 ks, 28.7 ks) with Manning 0.05 where the base case has none. S3 never switches.
  So: route choice is robust where the margin is large (S2, S3) and not where it is small (S1); incision magnitude is always closure-limited. This is a result about the model under the tested closures, not a validation of any closure.

## 3. Solver robustness near pathway activation
* The raw discharge law stalls at first overtopping in S1 geometry within a 60 000-evaluation cap (59 s) and so does the independent bisection implementation (240 s wall limit); a 0.1 mm floor also stalls; floors of 1 mm and 10 mm complete in 15 s and 5.6 s with identical event time (2584.13 s) and delivered volume (87.8464 m3 over 3 h). `docs/root_multiplicity.md`. The production floor (1 mm) is a numerical regularisation of a fold, not a physical rule.
* In the 35 frozen cases there were no stalls or failures.

## 4. Does the network activate new connections?
No. The edge list is declared before the run and never changes (`Network.edges`); no connection is created, relocated or removed. What occurs is (a) redistribution of the inflow between two predefined routes, (b) hydraulic activation of each predefined route when the stage passes its sill (R1 at t_act1 about 1 850-2 760 s, R2 at t_act2 about 3 530-4 830 s), and (c) melt-driven lowering of both sills that changes the split and, in S1/S2, produces the switch. This is "activation/deactivation of predefined connections", item 2 of the three-level classification, not pathway creation.

## 5. Laminar resolved benchmark (verification, not validation)
`ice_network_reference/graetz_benchmark.py`: a resolved 2-D laminar Graetz problem between plates is used to verify the exponential segment algebra and wall-area bookkeeping of the thermal march (segmentation independence 1e-15; the resolved solution recovers Nu 7.54096 against 7.5407; grid change 3e-4 at most). It also quantifies the entrance-region error of a constant-Nu closure in that laminar configuration (heat removal underestimated by 92 % at x* = 1e-4, 28 % at 0.1, 5 % at 0.5). **This verifies the stated laminar configuration only; it does not validate the turbulent, free-surface, melting-wall Dittus-Boelter closure used in production.**
